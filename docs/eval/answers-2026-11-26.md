# Answer eval, 2026-11-26

> Measured on the owner's Claude plan login before D34; later evals run on an API key.

This report holds numbers and ids only. The protocol that fixed the subset, the sweep, the gate and the decision rules before any run is in [answers-protocol.md](answers-protocol.md). The subset lock is [answer-subset.lock.json](answer-subset.lock.json), hash `749d7270636d`.

## Verdict

**Opus 5.5 at low effort with 2 extra calls meets the 4 s headline at p50 and keeps quality.** Its headline p50 is 3.3 s (95 percent interval 2.4 to 5.4 s). The old default, Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort, is 7.1 s (6.1 to 9.4 s). The paired difference is 4.14 s faster (3.02 to 6.80 s), which is outside the noise. It passes all six rules of the quality gate.

It does not meet 4 s for every question. 22 of its 36 answers show a headline within 4 s. Of the other 14, 10 searched again first, and the headline p50 of answers that searched is 12.3 s. 4 did not search and still took longer than 4 s. The searching tail is the next problem, and it is not a model or effort problem.

D14 holds. Across 260 answers no headline was taken back, and no answer needed a retry.

The engine default changes from Sonnet 5.5 medium to Opus 5.5 low in this PR. Nothing else in the engine changes.

## Why the headline is where it is

The time before the first text splits into retrieval, the wait for the process, and thinking. Retrieval is 0.1 to 0.2 s and the process wait is 0.0 s in every run, because the spare process was warm for all 260 answers. Thinking is the whole difference.

| Run | think p50 | first text to headline p50 | output tokens p50 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sonnet-medium-x2` | 6.4 (5.5 to 9.0) | 0.4 | 2,249 |
| `sonnet-low-x2` | 5.4 (1.5 to 7.3) | 0.4 | 1,946 |
| `haiku-medium-x2` | 12.0 (11.3 to 14.2) | 0.2 | 4,552 |
| `opus-low-x2` | 2.5 (1.6 to 4.5) | 0.5 | 1,630 |
| `opus-low-x1` | 1.6 (1.4 to 3.8) | 0.5 | 1,686 |
| `opus-low-x0` | 5.4 (3.2 to 6.3) | 0.6 | 2,232 |

- **Why not twice as good.** At `opus-low-x2` the first text arrives at 2.8 s. The earlier spike measured 1.9 s for a trivial prompt on a warm process (n=5), so about 1.9 s is the floor of this path and 0.9 s is left to gain. Effort is not the lever any more.
- **Haiku is slowest, not fastest.** It writes the most output tokens (4,552 at p50) and thinks the longest (12.0 s). Output tokens are the proxy for thinking. At about 190 tokens per second, the p50 of 2,249 tokens for `sonnet-medium-x2` is about 12 s of output, of which the visible JSON is a fraction.
- **Opus is not slower for being larger.** At low effort it thinks 2.5 s. The cause is a guess. Opus may budget less thinking at low effort than Sonnet does. This run did not test that.
- **Removing tools made the headline slower.** With no extra calls, `opus-low-x0` thinks 5.4 s, against 2.5 s at 2 calls. The paired difference is 3.30 s slower (0.32 to 4.08 s). The cause is a guess. The model may decide faster when it can search, and spend its thinking on the answer when it cannot. The "no tool" column is not comparable across the runs, because there the model chose which questions to answer without searching.

## Noise floor

- **Sampling.** Each interval above is a 95 percent bootstrap over the 40 questions (2,000 seeded resamples). The paired differences below resample the questions together, so a hard question is hard for both runs.
- **Paired differences in headline p50.** Against `sonnet-medium-x2`. `sonnet-low-x2` is 1.00 s faster (interval 4.29 s faster to 0.14 s slower, 34 questions), which is inside the noise. `haiku-medium-x2` is 5.23 s slower (3.51 to 7.66 s, 35 questions). `opus-low-x2` is 4.14 s faster (3.02 to 6.80 s, 35 questions). `opus-low-x1` is 4.41 s faster (3.62 to 7.05 s, 36 questions). `opus-low-x0` is 0.87 s faster (0.09 to 3.84 s, 34 questions), which only just clears.
- **Against `opus-low-x2`.** `opus-low-x1` is 0.40 s faster (1.26 s faster to 0.37 s slower, 35 questions), which is inside the noise. `opus-low-x0` is 3.30 s slower (0.32 to 4.08 s, 33 questions).
- **Same question, run twice.** 10 explicit questions ran again on each of two configurations. The gap between the two runs of the same question has a headline p50 of 1.99 s and a p90 of 5.20 s for `opus-low-x2` (8 questions with a headline both times). For `opus-low-x1` the p50 is 0.71 s and the p90 is 12.03 s. The large gap is one question that searched in one run and did not in the other. The done time has a p50 of 2.07 s and a p90 of 11.81 s for `opus-low-x2`, and 1.43 s and 14.02 s for `opus-low-x1`.
- **What follows.** `opus-low-x1` at 2.7 s and `opus-low-x2` at 3.3 s are not different. A gap under about 2 s is not a result. Whether the model searches is not repeatable from one run to the next for the same question.
- **Not measured.** Each configuration ran once, in one block, so time-of-day drift is not separated from configuration. The repeat runs came last and sit inside the interval of the first runs. The 40 questions are one sample, and a different 40 would shift the intervals.

## D14, the verifying headline

Zero visible restarts in 260 answers, and zero retried answers. The rule was to keep the verifying headline when at most 1 of the 40 answers in the chosen configuration restarts. `opus-low-x2` has 0 of 40. By the rule of three, 0 of 260 puts the rate under 1.2 percent at 95 percent confidence.

The headline shows at 3.3 s and settles at 5.2 s at p50 for `opus-low-x2`, so a reader sees "memeriksa…" for about 2 s. The recommendation is to keep it.

## Extra calls (D2, D2a)

- 11 to 17 of 40 answers searched again in every run that had a budget. They are where the headline is late. The headline p50 for an answer with extra calls is 12.3 s at `opus-low-x2`.
- At `opus-low-x2` the share that searched is highest for insufficient questions (3 of 4), then inferred (4 of 8), differing (1 of 5) and explicit (4 of 15). Fictional and revision questions made none.
- With 1 call, 11 answers use a call and 4 more calls are denied past the budget. With 2 calls, 12 answers use calls and 2 are denied. A budget of 1 is not a measurable gain on the headline, and a budget of 0 is slower.
- The queries of every call are in the traces under `data/traces/` (D2a). They hold question text and are not in this report.

## Limits of this eval

- **No revisions file.** `data/revisions.json` does not exist (D8), so no passage is superseded. The revision category tests only whether the model writes a `revision` note that cites two passages. All 4 answers have `answer` type in every run, and the note with two cited passages appears in 0 to 2 of 4 across runs (1 of 4 at `opus-low-x2`). The superseded-citation check had nothing to catch.
- **Basis labels.** The basis matches in 12 to 16 of 23 across runs. The model says `inferred` where the question was drafted as explicit in about half the cases. Whether the drafted label or the model is right is not decided here.
- **Insufficient questions.** Only 1 or 2 of 4 match in every run (see the three ids above). These need relabelling or a rewrite before they can separate models.
- **No judge of support.** Whether a cited passage supports its claim is not scored. A model judge was out of scope for this PR.
- **Invented references and the headline.** All 4 invented-reference questions list the reference in `unverified_references` in every run, and no answer cites a matching passage. A keyword scan of the headline for "not found" wording finds it in 2 of 4 at `sonnet-medium-x2`, 2 of 4 at `sonnet-low-x2`, and in none of the answers at `haiku-medium-x2` and the three Opus runs. This is a scan, not a reading. If the screen shows the headline before the unverified-reference list, an Opus reader sees the answer to the nearby real question first. The gate does not catch this.
- **Retrieval overlap.** The silver labels are in the table above and are not part of the decision.

## Recommendation

1. **Default model and effort.** Opus 5.5 at low effort with 2 extra calls. It is the only configuration with a headline p50 under 4 s (3.3 s), it passes the gate, and its quality equals the Sonnet runs within the 2-case tolerance. Sonnet 5.5 at low effort is the nearest alternative at 6.0 s and misses the target by 2 s.
2. **Extra calls.** Keep 2 (D2). A budget of 1 is not measurably faster and a budget of 0 is slower.
3. **D14.** Keep the verifying headline.
4. **Next problem.** The answers that search again take 12 to 13 s to a headline, and they are about 30 percent of answers. Fewer searches, or a visible "searching" state, would move p90. Both are outside this PR.
5. **Before the next run.** Relabel w-06, w-07 and q-d4d5fc52-1, and write `data/revisions.json` (D8) so the superseded check has something to catch.

Open question for the owner. Opus counts more against a Claude subscription's limits than Sonnet does. This eval does not measure how much more, only tokens, which are similar (65,889 output tokens for `opus-low-x2` against 82,753 for `sonnet-medium-x2`). If limits bind, Sonnet 5.5 at low effort is the fallback at the cost of the 4 s target.
